## Where things are

- **[The Brief](the-brief)**: four pages: what you want, the house as it stands, what it can cost, and what the permits require. Everything else gets checked against them.
- **[Rooms](rooms)**: one page per room the project touches, in folders by floor, then room, then an area or a fixture when a room needs more than one page (`main/kitchen/island`): what the finished space has to do.
- **[The Work](the-work)**: one page per job, in folders by phase in the order the work runs (Demolition, Structure, Rough-In, Close-Up, Finishes, Trim-Out): what gets done, and what each job waits on.
- **[Selections](selections)**: one page per fixture, finish, or material, in the same floor and room folders as Rooms: what it has to be, the options weighed against that, and what you chose.
- **[Change Orders](change-orders)**: one file per change to the scope after the quote was signed: what changed, why, and what it cost. You sign each one off; each is listed on the jobs and rooms it changes, and together they settle the final invoice.
- **[Files](files)**: drawings, permits, quotes, spec sheets, and site photos, each linked to the pages and tasks it belongs to. A set that travels together, such as the permit drawings or a bid with its attachments, is a bundle: one folder inside Files.

## The one-page docs

- **[Contacts](contacts.md)**: everyone on the job, on one page.

## The work on site

The pages say what the house will be; the Backlog tracks getting there. Its **On Site** board holds the tasks, the inspections, and the orders, each tagged with its phase and trade and linked to the job, room, or pick it serves, so every page lists its own under On Site. Its **Punch List** board holds the punch items from the walk-through: a trade marks one Fixed, and it stays open until you accept it. The saved views pick out what holds the job up: rough-in still open, inspections to book or pass, orders not yet in, and punch items waiting on your walk.

The canvas **The Order of the Work** draws the phases and the gates between them (the permit, the rough-in inspection, the final inspection, the punch walk) on one page.

## How to use it

A room's page says what the finished room **is**: why it is in the project, what it has to do, and what it does not get. A job's page says what gets **done** to get there, as a list a contractor can quote and you can walk through afterward. A pick's page says what a thing has to **be** before you buy it, and lists each option you weighed, with the one you chose signed off. None of them is a to-do list: that is the Backlog's. Write the page before the quote, and keep it true after: a room you drop stays here, marked `Cut`, so the reasoning survives the next conversation about it. A change after the quote is a change order, never an edit made quietly to a job's list.
