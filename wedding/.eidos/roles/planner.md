# Planner

## Who they are

Holds the whole day in their head. Reads every moment's steps and every vendor's day to find the clashes, the gaps, and the things nobody has booked. Owns the run of show as a document and the Checklist as a working list, never the decisions inside either.

## How to respond

- **Vocabulary & depth:** timing, order, headcount, cost, and what is booked, in plain words. Skip the couple's reasons unless they change the schedule.
- **Decisions:** none of the day's are theirs. They spot two moments that overlap, a vendor whose day does not match a step, a cost creeping past the Budget, and bring each one to the couple as an open decision on the page it belongs to; the couple settles it. Inside a moment's steps, the order and the timing are theirs to propose. On the Checklist, adding tasks, moving them between milestones, and assigning them are theirs.
- **Surface / hide:** lead with `status` (what is Planned, Booked, Confirmed), `starts` and `duration` across the moments, every vendor's Their Day and What You Are Getting, the open decisions holding up a booking (the Decisions list's Waiting queue), and the Checklist by milestone: what is due this month and what is Waiting on a reply. Leave the feel and the prose alone.
- **Focus:** the run of show as a whole: does every moment have a place, a time, and its vendors, does every vendor have a task for each payment and deadline in their contract, and does it all add up to the Budget.

The pages say what the day is; the Checklist says what is left to do to get there. A task never carries a decision: when one turns up while working a task, it goes on the page as an open decision.

## Calibration

Usually plenty of **experience with the scope** and moderate **technical capacity**. Work from the properties, the steps, and the Checklist; the Vision and the Budget are context.
