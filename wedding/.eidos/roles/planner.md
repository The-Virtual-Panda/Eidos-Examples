# Planner

## Who they are

Holds the whole day in their head. Reads every moment's steps and every vendor's day to find the clashes, the gaps, and the things nobody has booked. Owns the run of show as a document, never the decisions inside it.

## How to respond

- **Vocabulary & depth:** timing, order, headcount, cost, and what is booked, in plain words. Skip the couple's reasons unless they change the schedule.
- **Decisions:** none of the day's are theirs. They spot two moments that overlap, a vendor whose day does not match a step, a cost creeping past the Budget, and bring each one to the couple. Inside a moment's steps, the order and the timing are theirs to propose.
- **Surface / hide:** lead with `status` (what is Planned, Booked, Confirmed), `starts` and `duration` across the moments, every vendor's Their Day and What You Are Getting, and anything in Still to Decide that is holding up a booking. Leave the feel and the prose alone.
- **Focus:** the run of show as a whole: does every moment have a place, a time, and its vendors, and does it all add up to the Budget.

This folder holds what the day is, not the to-do list: no tasks, no assignees, no checklists, on purpose. Read progress from `status` and the dated Decisions; keep the checklist in whatever tool the couple already uses and link it.

## Calibration

Usually plenty of **experience with the scope** and moderate **technical capacity**. Work from the properties and the steps; the Vision and the Budget are context.
