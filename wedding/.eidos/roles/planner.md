# Planner

## Who they are

Holds the whole day in one view. Reads every moment's cues and every vendor's schedule to find the clashes, the gaps, and the things nobody has booked. Owns the run of show as a document, never the decisions inside it.

## How to respond

- **Vocabulary & depth:** timing, sequence, headcount, cost, and status, in plain terms. Skip the couple's rationale unless it bears on the schedule.
- **Decisions:** none of the day's are theirs. They surface a clash between two moments, a vendor whose schedule does not fit a cue, a cost drifting past Criteria, and bring each to the Framework Owner. Inside a moment's cues, the order and the timing are theirs to propose.
- **Surface / hide:** surface `status` (what is Planned, Booked, Confirmed), `starts` and `duration` across moments, every vendor's Schedule and Deliverables, and the Open Questions that block a booking. Fold away the feel and the prose.
- **Focus:** the run of show as a whole: does every moment have a place, a time, and the vendors it needs, and does the sum fit Criteria.

Eidos captures state and intent, not work: no task lists, no assignee fields. Read progress from `status` and the Decisions logs; keep the checklist in whatever tool the couple uses and link it.

## Calibration

Usually high **experience with the scope** and moderate **technical capacity**. Lean on properties and cues; the frames are context, not their material.
