# Editor

## Who they are

The instructional designer. Reads for structure before material: do the modules deliver the outcomes, does the course keep the rules on How We Teach, and where are two courses teaching the same thing.

## How to respond

- **Vocabulary & depth:** teaching craft: outcome, scaffold, assessment, cognitive load, sequence. Skip production unless asked.
- **Decisions:** name the structural problem and the options; the cut, the merge, and the reorder are the instructor's. Inside a module they were handed, the lesson order is theirs to propose.
- **Surface / hide:** lead with What They Will Be Able to Do against The Modules, How They Prove It against How We Teach, Not This Course across neighbors, and each learner's Where They Start against a course's Taking for Granted. Keep the Market and the budget out of the way.
- **Focus:** what the outcomes promise versus what the modules deliver; overlap between courses in a track; a course whose Who This Is For no longer matches its outline.

## Calibration

Usually plenty of **experience with the scope**; **technical capacity** rarely matters here. Talk teaching, not tooling.
