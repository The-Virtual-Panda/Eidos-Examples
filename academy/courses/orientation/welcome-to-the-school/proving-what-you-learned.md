# Proving What You Learned

## What It Serves

- [LO3](welcome-to-the-school.md#lo3): a learner can take a quiz as practice or as an official attempt, and say which one counts.
- [LO4](welcome-to-the-school.md#lo4): a learner can study a flashcard deck until each card is learned.

## What They Take Away

- A lesson is checked by a **quiz**: a short set of questions drawn from the lesson's own page. Questions come in seven kinds, from multiple choice and true or false to putting steps in order and writing an answer in a few sentences.
- A quiz can be taken two ways. **Practice** stays on the learner's own machine and can be repeated as often as they like. An **official attempt** is shared with the school and kept in the learner's history; it is what counts toward their record.
- Each question shows **why** its answer is right the moment it is answered, practice or official.
- A **written answer** is graded by a person: the quiz's creator, a proctor, or one of the school's owners. An official attempt with a written answer counts toward no score until every written answer in it is graded.
- A **flashcard deck** is studied a few cards at a time, whenever the learner likes. A card is learned once they answer **Got it** twice in a row. A deck has no score and no due dates.
- Quizzes and decks sit in order in a **course** in Academy, named for the course they check. A learner **enrolls** in it to follow their progress across it; nobody has to enroll to take a quiz or study a deck.
- Scoring runs on good faith: the answers can be read, and the school keeps results rather than policing them.

## What They Do

Take the first lesson's quiz once as practice, then once as an official attempt, and compare what each one kept. Then study this lesson's deck until every card is learned.

## How It Is Checked

The quiz [Proving What You Learned](../../../academy/welcome-to-the-school/proving-what-you-learned.md) in Academy, second in the course [Welcome to the School](../../../academy/welcome-to-the-school/course.md): six questions, the last a written answer a person grades. Then the deck [Words for Proving It](../../../academy/welcome-to-the-school/words-for-proving-it.md), six cards to keep.
