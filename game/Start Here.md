## Where things are

- **[The Game](<The Game>)**: five pages: the pillars and the core loop, who plays, where it runs, how much game the team can finish, and where it sits in the market. Everything else gets checked against them.
- **[Mechanics](Mechanics)**: one page per mechanic, grouped by system: why it is in the game, how it plays, its rules and numbers.
- **[Levels](Levels)**: one page per level, grouped by act: what it teaches, the flow, what the player leaves with.
- **[Characters](Characters)**: one page per character the player meets, plays, or fights, enemies and bosses included.
- **[Items](Items)**: one page per item, upgrade, or collectible, grouped by the same systems as the mechanics.
- **[Files](Files)**: concept art, blockouts, maps, balance sheets, prototypes, and the pitch deck the pages link to.

## The one-page docs

- **[The One-Pager](<The One-Pager.md>)**: the whole game on one page, for a publisher, a festival, or a new teammate.
- **[Cut List](<Cut List.md>)**: everything cut from the game, and why.
- **[Playtest Log](<Playtest Log.md>)**: every playtest, dated: the build, who played, what you saw, what changed.
- **[Art Bible](<Art Bible.md>)**: how the game looks and sounds.

## How to use it

A page here says what one piece of the game **is**: why it is in the game, which pillar it serves, how it plays, its rules and numbers, and what it is not. It is not a task list and not a script: the build is where the game gets made, and this is what the build is checked against, by the team and by a coding agent if you work with one. Every page starts as a one-pager; grow it into the full page when it earns it. Write the page before the prototype and keep it true after: a number you tuned is a dated decision, and a mechanic you cut stays here, marked `Cut` with its `cut_reason`, so nobody builds it again by accident.
