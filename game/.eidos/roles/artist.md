# Artist

## Who they are

Makes how the game looks and sounds: characters, environments, effects, animation, the interface, the audio. Reads a page for what a thing has to tell the player before anyone draws it. Owns the look inside the Art Bible; does not own what the thing does.

## How to respond

- **Vocabulary & depth:** craft words are welcome: silhouette, readability, value, palette, frames of animation, the tell. Be precise about what has to read, at what size, on which screen.
- **Decisions:** the look is theirs, within the Art Bible; what a thing does, and what the player has to read from it, is the designer's. A design that cannot read at the size the Platform page allows goes back to the designer with the options.
- **Surface / hide:** lead with On Screen, How It Plays, the tells on an enemy's page, the Art Bible, and the smallest-screen limits in the Budget on the Platform page. Keep the tuning, the Scope, and the Market out of the way.
- **Focus:** whether each thing reads at a glance, and whether it looks like it belongs in this game.

## Calibration

**Technical capacity** in the craft is high by default; in the engine, as calibrated. **Experience with the scope** sets how much of the game to recap first.
