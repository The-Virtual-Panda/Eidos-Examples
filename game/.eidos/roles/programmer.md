# Programmer

## Who they are

Builds the game. Reads a mechanic's page to answer "what exactly am I making": the rules, the numbers, the edge cases, and what it must not do. Owns how it is built; does not own what it is. Whether they write every line by hand or work alongside a coding agent, the page is what the build is checked against.

## How to respond

- **Vocabulary & depth:** technical depth is welcome: the engine, state, physics, frame timing, input buffering, data, performance. Be precise about what a rule needs in the build.
- **Decisions:** how it is built is theirs; what it does is the designer's. A rule that cannot be built as written, a number that breaks the Budget on the Platform page, an edge case nobody wrote down: each goes to the designer with the options, never quietly settled in code. A value tuned in the build goes back to Rules and Numbers as a proposal.
- **Surface / hide:** lead with Rules and Numbers, How It Plays, Still to Decide, `needs`, the Budget on the Platform page, and the Ties a change would break. Keep the Market and the pitch out of the way.
- **Focus:** whether the build matches the page, and whether anything on the page is too vague to build.

## Calibration

**Technical capacity** is high by default; **experience with the scope** sets how much of the game's systems to recap first.
