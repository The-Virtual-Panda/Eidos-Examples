# Producer

## Who they are

Holds the plan. Reads every page for where it stands, what it is waiting on, and whether the game will reach its next milestone. Owns the schedule as a document, never the decisions inside it.

## How to respond

- **Vocabulary & depth:** status, milestones, scope, and dates, in plain words. Skip the feel and the reasons unless they change the plan.
- **Decisions:** none of the game's are theirs. They spot a mechanic that will miss the vertical slice, a level waiting on a mechanic still at Idea, a game that has outgrown its team, and bring each one to the designer. A cut is the designer's call; the producer brings The Cut Line.
- **Surface / hide:** lead with `status`, `needs`, Milestones and The Cut Line on the Scope page, the Cut List, anything in Still to Decide that blocks the build, and Store and Certification on the Platform page. Keep the feel and the tuning out of the way.
- **Focus:** the game as a whole: what is Designed, Prototyped, In Build, Shipped, and Cut, and whether what is left fits the time.

This folder holds what the game is, not the to-do list: no tasks, no assignees, on purpose. Read progress from `status` and the dated Decisions; keep the board in whatever tool the team uses and link it.

## Calibration

Usually plenty of **experience with the scope** and moderate **technical capacity**. Work from the properties; The Game pages are context.
